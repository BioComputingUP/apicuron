About APICURON

Introduction

Biocuration plays an essential role in making research data available to the scientific community and shareable, therefore increasing their intrinsic value. However, the contribution and effort of biocurators are extremely difficult to quantify and to acknowledge. This is relevant not only in the case of hired-curators but especially in the case of databases relying on community curation. APICURON provides partner biological databases with a real-time automatic tracking system of the biocuration activities, allowing them to acknowledge and recognize the work of their biocurators. APICURON is based on the key concept of gamification, therefore relying on a system of badges and scores that are used to recognize the work of biocurators and at the same time encourage and motivate them.

APICURON is highly flexible and allows partner databases to define their own list of badges and rules to assign them.

Biocuration activities

The description of a curation event in APICURON is provided by four key components: entity, activity, agent and timestamp. These key elements fulfill the recommendation of Thessen et al.

  • Entity: it corresponds to the curated data object, which is identified by using the identifiers.org resolving system.
  • Activity: this is the actual curation activity, such as “entry_creation” or “evidence_update”. Although different databases can define different activities, those need to be mapped to PROV term PROV is a W3C recommendation that provides a way to define the life cycle of an object. PROV terms are: generation, revision and invalidation.
  • Agent: the biocurator that has performed the biocuration activity. In APICURON biocurators are identified by their ORCID, allowing them to make their contributions citable.
  • Timestamp: it provides the information, i.e. the date and time of the day, of when a biocuration activity was performed. Each curation report is accessible by users via API.
A list of the entity and activity terms for each one of our partner databases is available.

Badges and scores

Gamification is a trending topic that consists of providing motivational affordances and “gameful” experiences in non-game related settings. In APICURON it is used to support biocurators’ engagement by acknowledging their work and effort. Badges and scores are assigned to each biocurator based on their achievements, as a positive feedback mechanism that keeps users engaged and motivated and increases their competitiveness.

A list of the badges and scores applied by each one of our partner databases is available.

How long do we keep your personal data?

Any personal data directly obtained from you will be retained as long as the service is live, even if you stop using the service. We will keep the personal data for the minimum amount of time possible to ensure legal compliance and to facilitate internal and external audits if they arise. Access to the web and security logs containing personal data are restricted to relevant APICURON staff.

Your rights regarding your personal data

You have the right to:

  1. Not be subject to decisions based solely on an automated processing of data (i.e. without human intervention) without you having your views taken into consideration.
  2. Request at reasonable intervals and without excessive delay or expense, information about the personal data processed about you. Under your request we will inform you in writing about, for example, the origin of the personal data or the preservation period.
  3. Request information to understand data processing activities when the results of these activities are applied to you.
  4. Object at any time to the processing of your personal data unless we can demonstrate that we have legitimate reasons to process your personal data.
  5. Request free of charge and without excessive delay rectification or erasure of your personal data.

It must be clarified that rights 4 and 5 are only available whenever the processing of your personal data is not necessary to:

  • Comply with a legal obligation.
  • Perform a task carried out in the public interest.
  • Exercise authority as a data controller.
  • Archive for purposes in the public interest, or for historical research purposes, or for statistical purposes.
  • Establish, exercise or defend legal claims.
